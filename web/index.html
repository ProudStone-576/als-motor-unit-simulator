Hidden phase

How much is gone by the first symptom?

When a neuron dies its fibres shrink (magenta). Surviving neighbours adopt them, so fibres of one type start to cluster: the “fibre-type grouping” pathologists see in ALS.

Virtual patient

What happens in the hidden phase?

Compensation hides the damage, and a blood test can see it. Surviving neurons grow to cover for lost ones, so strength holds until compensation runs out. Neurofilament light (NfL), a protein released by dying axons, tracks the rate of loss instead, so it rises long before symptoms.

What if we treat?

Same patient, same drug, different timing. Pick how a treatment works and when it starts. Drugs that slow degeneration lower NfL within weeks, but function separates only months later. A drug that only boosts strength moves function at once and never touches NfL.

How the treatment works

Would a real trial notice?

Hundreds of virtual patients are screened like a real trial, and each eligible one is simulated with and without the drug. Thousands of randomised trials are then replayed, with dropouts and measurement noise, to estimate how often each endpoint would show the effect.

How does the model work?

A mechanism you can read, test and change. Everything above comes from one open model running in your browser. It mirrors the Python package line by line, and an automated test checks that both give the same numbers.

MechanismWhat the model doesSource
120 motor units per regionArranged by Henneman’s size principle: small slow units are recruited first, big fast ones last.Fuglevand et al. 1993
Biggest neurons fail firstFast-fatigable units are the most vulnerable and sprout the least.Frey et al. 2000; Pun et al. 2006
Dying backA neuron first loses its grip on the muscle, then dies.Fischer et al. 2004
ReinnervationSurvivors adopt orphaned fibres up to a size limit; fibres left orphaned too long are lost for good.
Upper motor neuronsTheir loss weakens the drive to muscles, and their glutamate signalling stresses lower motor neurons.
Spread and blood NfLDisease spreads to neighbouring regions. Dying axons release NfL in proportion to their size and length.Ravits & La Spada 2009

Limits. Parameters are illustrative and tuned to population averages (median survival about 2.5 to 3 years, bulbar onset worst, about half the neurons gone at symptom onset). The model has no cognition, nutrition or ventilation care, and survival is more predictable than in real life. Use it to build intuition and test ideas, never to predict someone’s course.
